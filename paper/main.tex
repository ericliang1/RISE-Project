\begin{abstract}
When a gas leak or pollution release happens, responders must answer a deceptively
simple question: \emph{where is it coming from?} --- increasingly from only a handful of
cheap, fixed sensors. A learned localizer can output a heatmap of likely sources, but a
heatmap alone does not say how much to trust it. We introduce a benchmark for
uncertainty-aware source localization whose defining asset is the \emph{exact Bayesian
posterior} of every scenario, shipped with the data, and show that this asset does double
duty. \textbf{First, it audits.} Pairing split conformal prediction with the exact
posteriors yields a preregistered three-way test of any localizer: calibration (do $90\%$
regions cover $90\%$?), sharpness (are regions as tight as the data physically permit?),
and a causal information dose--response (do regions track added sensors, geometry, and
noise the way the physics dictates?). On a standard DeepSets baseline the verdict is
differentiated: calibrated (conformal-on-exact-posterior attains $90.7\%$; the learned
localizer $89.6\%$, with $87.5\%$ for a second seed quantified as a finite-sample
fluctuation), but a median $5.6\times$ too wide --- the network, not the data, is the
bottleneck --- and directionally correct on every knob at only half the oracle's magnitude.
\textbf{Second, it trains.} Using the same shipped posteriors as a \emph{physics-derived
training signal} --- distilling each scenario's exact posterior, mildly tempered, into the
identical architecture on identical data --- sharpens regions to $3.9\times$ and nearly
restores oracle-proportional sensor response ($0.47 \to 0.87$ slope ratio); raw
(untempered) physics targets fail, an optimization finding in its own right. The study
also surfaced a floating-point failure mode that silently destroys conformal coverage on
sharp probability maps, among other portable implementation lessons. To our knowledge this
is the first benchmark in this domain to ship exact per-scenario posteriors, the first to
pair finite-sample conformal regions with an exact-posterior sharpness audit, and the
first to test learned localization uncertainty causally through a controlled
dose--response protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Locating the source of an airborne pollutant from a sparse sensor network is a classic
inverse problem whose legal stakes are rising. Under the U.S.\ EPA Super-Emitter Program,
certified third parties notify operators of remotely detected methane events ($\geq$100
kg/hr), and an operator within 50 m of the reported coordinates must investigate and report
[U.S. EPA, 2024]; EU Regulation 2024/1787 mandates measurement, reporting, and verification
of energy-sector methane [European Union, 2024]; and UNEP's Methane Alert and Response
System delivered 1{,}200 satellite-based notifications in its first two years --- of which
one per cent were responded to [UNEP, 2024]. When attributing an emission to a
\emph{location} carries consequences, the \emph{uncertainty} of that attribution must
itself be trustworthy: a localizer that is confidently wrong is worse than one that
honestly reports ``somewhere in this larger region.''

Three properties matter. \textbf{Calibration} (trustworthiness): a claimed $90\%$ region
should contain the true source $90\%$ of the time. \textbf{Sharpness} (actionability):
among calibrated regions, smaller is better, and a region should be as tight as the data
physically allow --- a large region is itself a signal that the sensor network is
inadequate. \textbf{Dose--response stability} (trust under change): sensors are added and
lost constantly, so a region should shrink when information arrives and grow when it is
removed, tracking what an oracle would do.

\paragraph{Contributions.} Three, in order of emphasis:
\textbf{(1) A benchmark whose central asset is exact posteriors.} A controlled
advection--diffusion simulator with an analytic Green's-function forward model, a frozen
14{,}000-scenario benchmark, constructed dose--response sets --- and, for every scenario,
the \emph{exact grid posterior} of the source location, shipped with the artifact. The
posteriors power a preregistered three-way audit of any localizer: finite-sample
conformal calibration, a sharpness audit against the physical information limit, and a
causal information dose--response protocol.
\textbf{(2) A preregistered audit of a standard recipe.} We deliberately assemble
off-the-shelf components --- DeepSets [Zaheer et al., 2017] and split conformal prediction
[Angelopoulos and Bates, 2021] --- and measure whether the resulting uncertainty is
calibrated, sharp, and stable (it is calibrated; it is not sharp; Sec.~\ref{sec:h1}--\ref{sec:h3}).
\textbf{(3) The posteriors double as a training signal.} Distilling each scenario's exact
posterior --- tempered to a learnable sharpness --- into the \emph{identical} architecture
on \emph{identical} data substantially improves every uncertainty-quality metric the audit
measures (exploratory, validation-selected; Secs.~\ref{sec:distillmethod},
\ref{sec:distill}).
Verbatim: \emph{To our knowledge, this is the first public benchmark in this domain to
ship exact per-scenario posteriors, the first study to combine finite-sample conformal
spatial regions with an exact-posterior sharpness audit for sparse-sensor atmospheric
advection--diffusion source localization, and the first to test learned localization
uncertainty causally via a controlled information dose--response protocol against exact
posteriors.} We claim no new conformal algorithm, no new architecture, no coverage under
distribution shift, no field deployability, and no abstention capability; the distillation
recipe is an application of teacher--student learning whose teacher is exact physics
rather than another network.

\paragraph{Findings at a glance.}
(i)~\textbf{H1:} the conformal machinery is sound --- applied to the exact posterior it
attains $90.7\%$ coverage; the learned localizer attains $89.6\%$ (seed 2) and $87.5\%$
(seed 1, a quantified $p = 0.0045$ finite-sample fluctuation; Sec.~\ref{sec:h1}).
(ii)~\textbf{H2:} calibrated is not sharp --- learned regions are a median $5.6\times$
larger than the data permit; the network, not the data, is the bottleneck
(Sec.~\ref{sec:h2}).
(iii)~\textbf{H3:} the learned regions respond in the physically correct direction on all
three information knobs, at roughly half the oracle's magnitude (Sec.~\ref{sec:h3}).
(iv)~\textbf{The benchmark trains as well as it audits:} distilling its shipped exact
posteriors --- mildly tempered --- into the \emph{same} model on the \emph{same} data
sharpens regions to $3.9\times$ and lifts the sensor-response slope ratio from $0.47$ to
$0.87$; raw physics targets fail (Secs.~\ref{sec:distillmethod}, \ref{sec:distill}).
(v)~Three implementation lessons that transfer beyond this benchmark: the standard
conformal score silently saturates in floating point on sharp heatmaps; fixed-node rate
quadrature cannot marginalize peaked likelihoods; and at this data scale the specified
architecture memorizes unless the problem's exact symmetry group is injected as
augmentation (Secs.~\ref{sec:conformal}--\ref{sec:oracle}, \ref{sec:deepsets}).

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Source term estimation and Bayesian inversion.} Estimating the location and rate of an
atmospheric release is reviewed by Hutchinson et al.\ [2017]. Bayesian/MCMC plume inversion
[Newman et al., 2024] quantifies uncertainty rigorously but is per-scenario and heavy, and
its credibility depends on the assumed dispersion model. Physics-informed neural networks
recover advection--diffusion sources [Chuprov et al., 2025, Anague et al., 2025] but
provide no finite-sample coverage. ``Information density'' in inverse problems [Bangerth
et al., 2022] formalizes why some regions are recoverable and others are not.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction for localization and inverse problems.} Conformal prediction
[Angelopoulos and Bates, 2021] has recently reached localization: acoustic source
localization with Gaussian processes [Rozenfeld and Laufer-Goldshtein, 2025]; task-driven
UQ for imaging inverse problems [Wen et al., 2024]; multi-source detection on networks
[Jian et al., 2026]; anatomical landmark localization [Jonkers et al., 2025]. Edwards and
Smith [2025] treat radiological plume identification with categorical and Bayesian
networks. Distill-Belief [Shi et al., 2026] distills a Bayes-correct particle-filter
teacher for closed-loop source localization --- closest in spirit to our post-hoc
physics-distilled variant (Sec.~\ref{sec:distill}). Closest in machinery, CP4SBI [Cabezas
et al., 2025] conformally calibrates simulation-based-inference credible sets, but with no
atmospheric domain, no exact-posterior sharpness audit, and no dose--response protocol.

\paragraph{Positioning.} Along five axes --- (A) amortized inference, (B) finite-sample region
coverage, (C) audit vs.\ an exact/reference posterior, (D) controlled causal test of
uncertainty, (E) sparse atmospheric sensors --- prior work occupies subsets
(Table~\ref{tab:positioning}); this study is the first to occupy all five simultaneously.

\section{Problem Setup and Physics}

A scalar concentration $C(\boldsymbol{x}, t)$ on $\Omega = [0, 1]^2$, $t \in (0, 1]$, is
transported by a constant wind $\boldsymbol{u} = (u, v)$ and isotropic diffusion $D$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial C}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial C}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial C}{\partial y}
= D \left( \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial y^2} \right) + S(\boldsymbol{x}, t),
\qquad \boldsymbol{x} \in \Omega,\ t \in (0, 1].
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
For a continuous point release at $\boldsymbol{x}_s$ switched on at $t = 0$, regularized by a small
source blob of width $\sigma_s = 1/128$, the unit-rate response is the superposition of
spreading, advected Gaussians:
\begin{equation}
A(\boldsymbol{x}, t; \boldsymbol{x}_s, \boldsymbol{u}, D)
= \int_0^t \frac{1}{\pi\, w_s} \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert \boldsymbol{x} - \boldsymbol{x}_s - \boldsymbol{u}\, s\rVert^2}{w_s}\right) \mathrm{d}s,
\qquad w_s = 4 D s + 2\sigma_s^2 .
\label{eq:greens}
\end{equation}
The age integral is evaluated by 64-node Gauss--Legendre quadrature after the log-age
substitution $s = t\, e^{-\tau}$, $\tau \in [0, 12]$ (bound tuned empirically;
Appendix~\ref{app:quadrature}); responses are generated in \texttt{float32} and validated
in \texttt{float64}. A release of rate $q$ produces field $q A$.

\paragraph{Observation model.} Sensor $i$ at $\boldsymbol{x}_i$ takes 30 readings at $t_k = k/30$:
\begin{equation}
y_{ik} = \frac{q\, A(\boldsymbol{x}_i, t_k; \boldsymbol{x}_s, \boldsymbol{u}, D)}{\Cref} + \varepsilon_{ik},
\qquad \varepsilon_{ik} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2),
\label{eq:obs}
\end{equation}
with independent Bernoulli reading dropout; dropped readings are \emph{omitted}, never
imputed, so the model must handle variable-length inputs. The data-generation prior is in
Table~\ref{tab:prior}. Splits are by \emph{complete scenario} to prevent leakage
(Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\section{Method}

\subsection{Amortized posterior (DeepSets classifier)}
\label{sec:deepsets}

The localizer is a permutation-invariant DeepSets network [Zaheer et al., 2017] of
4{,}869{,}568 parameters, chosen deliberately as a standard, unremarkable architecture.
Each retained reading is tokenized as
$\boldsymbol{z}_{ik} = [\, \mathrm{Fourier}(x_i), \mathrm{Fourier}(y_i),
\mathrm{Fourier}(t_k), \operatorname{asinh}(y_{ik}/\sigma) \,]$
with frequencies $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$. A per-token MLP ($\to 256 \to 256$, GELU + LayerNorm)
embeds each token; embeddings are pooled by \emph{both} masked mean and masked max,
concatenated; a context MLP ($5 \to 64 \to 64$) encodes $(u, v, \log D, \log \sigma, N)$;
a decoder ($576 \to 1024 \to 4096$) produces a softmax $P_j(\cdot)$ over a $64 \times 64$
grid. Training minimizes cross-entropy to a smoothed target (Gaussian bump, std 0.75
cells, truncated at $3\sigma$, centered on the true source): AdamW, lr $3\times10^{-4}$
cosine-decayed to $3\times10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 256, $\leq 200$ epochs,
early stopping on validation NLL (patience 25), mixed precision, two seeds. Because
targets are sampled from the prior, the softmax is an \emph{amortized approximation to the
grid posterior} $p(c \mid \boldsymbol{y})$.

\paragraph{Amendment: symmetry augmentation (deviation).}
As preregistered, this recipe \emph{memorizes}: training NLL falls to 1.5 while validation
NLL never beats the uniform level ($\approx 7.96$; $\log 4096 = 8.32$) and validation MAP
error (0.44) is worse than the peak-sensor baseline (0.37) --- verified not attributable to
mixed precision, learning rate, or the context branch. We therefore apply to each batch a
random D4 symmetry of the unit square (rotation $k \cdot 90^{\circ}$ + reflection) jointly
to sensors, wind, and source --- an \emph{exact} physics symmetry (validated to
$2\times10^{-13}$) under which the prior is invariant, so augmented scenarios are exact
prior draws. Nothing else changes; validation NLL then tracks training loss with no
overfitting gap. Finding: at this data scale the benchmark \emph{requires} symmetry
augmentation to be learnable by the specified architecture.

\subsection{Conformal regions}
\label{sec:conformal}

With $P_j$ the heatmap for calibration scenario $j$ and $c^\star_j$ the true cell, we use
the randomized highest-density-mass score
\begin{equation}
s_j \;=\; \sum_{c:\, P_j(c) > P_j(c^\star_j)} P_j(c) \;+\; U_j \cdot \sum_{c:\, P_j(c) = P_j(c^\star_j)} P_j(c),
\qquad U_j \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 1).
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
With $n = 2000$ and $\alpha = 0.10$, the threshold $\hat{q}$ is the
$\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil$-th smallest score; the region is the smallest set of
top-ranked cells whose cumulative mass reaches $\hat{q}$. Under exchangeability,
$\Pr( c^\star \in \mathcal{C}_\alpha(\boldsymbol{y}) ) \geq 1 - \alpha$
--- a guarantee that is \emph{marginal}, \emph{in-distribution}, \emph{not conditional},
and does \emph{not} survive distribution shift.

\paragraph{Amendment: numerically safe implementation.}
For sharp heatmaps, $s_j = 1 - \epsilon$ with $\epsilon$ down to $10^{-30}$ rounds to
exactly $1.0$ (catastrophic cancellation), creating an atom of tied scores that silently
breaks the region rule: conformal-on-exact-posterior covered $0.84$ instead of $0.90$
when Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} was evaluated naively. We compute throughout with the complement
tail mass $t_j = 1 - s_j$ (exact in exact arithmetic, representable at both ends ---
$t$-thresholds reach $4\times10^{-199}$), building regions by excluding the largest
ascending-mass tail. Coverage semantics are unchanged. Caution: this failure mode awaits
any conformal pipeline over sharp probability maps.

\subsection{Exact-posterior oracle}
\label{sec:oracle}

For each candidate cell $c$ with unit-rate response $\boldsymbol{g}_c$ at the known meteorology,
$p(\boldsymbol{y} \mid c, q) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{y};\ q \boldsymbol{g}_c,\ \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$; the rate is
marginalized over LogUniform$(0.5, 5)$ and the posterior normalized over all 4096 cells:
\begin{equation}
p(\boldsymbol{y} \mid c) = \int p(\boldsymbol{y} \mid c, q)\, \pi(q)\, \mathrm{d}q,
\qquad
p(c \mid \boldsymbol{y}) = \frac{p(\boldsymbol{y} \mid c)}{\sum_{c'} p(\boldsymbol{y} \mid c')}.
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
This oracle uses the true meteorology and noise level: it is the tightest honest region
the data permit, and the reference for H2/H3.

\paragraph{Amendment: peak-aware rate quadrature (deviation).}
The preregistered fixed 32-node Gauss--Legendre rule in $\log q$ is inadequate: the
integrand is a Gaussian in $q$ of width $\sigma / \lVert \boldsymbol{g}_c \rVert$ --- up to
$\sim\!1000\times$ narrower than the node spacing --- yielding log-posterior errors of many
nats. We split $[0.5, 5]$ per cell at $\hat{q}_c \pm 8\,\sigma/\lVert \boldsymbol{g}_c\rVert$
($\hat{q}_c = \langle \boldsymbol{y}, \boldsymbol{g}_c\rangle / \lVert \boldsymbol{g}_c \rVert^2$) with 32 peak +
$2{\times}16$ outer nodes, in \texttt{float64} log-space; validated against a $10\times$
deep rule and a 200{,}000-node trapezoid to $<10^{-6}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sanity}).

\subsection{Dose--response protocol}
\label{sec:dose}

We manipulate information content on constructed scenario sets and measure how learned and
exact region areas respond. \textbf{Knob 1 (sensor count):} nested subsets
$N = 4, \ldots, 12$ of a fixed 12-sensor master layout in a fixed insertion order --- each
added sensor is a controlled intervention. \textbf{Knob 2 (geometry):} matched pairs at
$N = 6$, stratified well-spread vs.\ confined single-quadrant (directional ambiguity).
\textbf{Knob 3 (noise):} a $\sigma$ sweep across $[0.01, 0.10]$ with regenerated readings.
All manipulations stay in-support; dose--response sets use $p_{\mathrm{drop}} = 0$ so
interventions are unconfounded. \emph{Wording discipline:} constructed layouts are not
exchangeable prior draws, so we report region-\emph{size} behavior only on these sets; the
coverage claim lives exclusively on the random test split.

\subsection{Physics-likelihood distillation: the posteriors as a training signal}
\label{sec:distillmethod}

The exact posteriors of Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle} are not only an audit reference --- they are
the Bayes-optimal \emph{training target}. In the baseline recipe each scenario teaches the
network one thing (``the source was here''); the posterior target teaches the full answer,
including its shape --- the downwind ambiguity ridge, the multimodal arc behind a confined
sensor cluster, the tightening that an extra sensor should produce. We therefore train the
\emph{identical} architecture, on the \emph{identical} 10k scenarios, with the identical
optimizer, schedule, and augmentation, replacing the smoothed one-hot target with the
scenario's exact posterior (Eq.~\eqref{eq:posterior}) --- \emph{tempered} by the same
0.75-cell Gaussian used for the baseline target; under D4 augmentation the target grid is
permuted by the exact same symmetry. Two implementation rules proved make-or-break:
(i) \textbf{temper the teacher} --- raw, near-delta posterior targets are an optimization
trap (validation region area $0.197$, worse than the baseline); (ii) \textbf{early-stop on
validation teacher cross-entropy}, not true-cell NLL --- the sharp teacher has poor
true-cell NLL by construction, and the mismatched criterion truncates training and
\emph{worsens} sharpness ($10.8\times$). Five loss variants (mixture weight
$\lambda \in \{1, 0.75, 0.5\}$; raw or tempered teacher) were compared by a pre-stated
validation-only rule (minimum median validation $90\%$-HPD area); the test split was
evaluated once, for the winner (tempered teacher, $\lambda = 1$). This recipe is
exploratory (not preregistered) and requires a tractable per-scenario posterior at
training time --- available by construction wherever training data come from a simulator,
as here and in simulation-based inference generally.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Preregistered Analyses}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Baselines.} (i) A \emph{peak-sensor heuristic}: point estimate at the sensor with the
largest time-integrated concentration. (ii) The \emph{exact posterior} (oracle).

\paragraph{Preregistered hypotheses.} \textbf{H1 (calibration):} conformal regions attain verified
$90\%$ marginal coverage on the test split (exact Clopper--Pearson $95\%$ interval).
\textbf{H2 (sharpness):} learned region area tracks exact-posterior area at matched
coverage --- the identical conformal procedure is applied to the exact posteriors, and we
report the per-scenario Spearman correlation and the mean/median area ratio
(\emph{inefficiency factor}). \textbf{H3 (dose--response):} under each knob, learned area
responds monotonically in the same direction and comparable proportion as exact area
(contraction-slope ratios; per-knob Spearman). \textbf{D1 (descriptive):} learned area
vs.\ MAP error. Headline metrics: MAP error; coverage@90\% with CP interval; region area;
H2 Spearman + inefficiency; H3 slope ratios + correlations. Appendix-only: NLL, JSD,
percentiles, $80\%/95\%$ levels, latency.

\paragraph{Validation gates.} Quadrature convergence; FD solver cross-check ($<1\%$ rel.\ $L_2$);
symmetry/linearity; exact-posterior sanity (normalization, $\sigma \to 0$ mode recovery,
broad posteriors for poor layouts, conformal-on-exact at nominal coverage, monotone area
in $q$ and $\sigma$, brute-force likelihood cross-check); scenario-ID leakage audit;
$96 \times 96$ grid refinement. All measured values in Appendices~\ref{app:quadrature}--\ref{app:refine}.

\paragraph{Deviations from the preregistration (complete list).}
Each forced by a correctness failure or ill-posedness caught by the pipeline's own
blocking gates: (i) D4 augmentation (Sec.~\ref{sec:deepsets}); (ii) tail-space conformal
scores (Sec.~\ref{sec:conformal}); (iii) peak-aware rate quadrature
(Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle}); (iv) sanity-check well-posedness --- cell-centered sanity
sources, identifiability conditioning, $\sigma = 10^{-6}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sanity});
(v) grid refinement on the full test split (100-scenario windows are too noisy:
coverage $0.79$--$0.97$; Appendix~\ref{app:refine}); (vi) absolute-error FD gating for
no-signal scenarios (Appendix~\ref{app:quadrature}). No hypothesis, metric, or split was
changed after seeing data. The physics-distilled variant of Sec.~\ref{sec:distill} is a
clearly-labeled post-hoc \emph{addition}, selected on validation only.

\subsection{H1: Calibration}
\label{sec:h1}

Test coverage is $0.875$ $[0.860, 0.889]$ for seed 1 --- the target $0.90$ is \emph{not}
contained --- and $0.896$ $[0.882, 0.909]$ for seed 2, which does contain it; the identical
procedure on the exact posterior attains $0.907$ $[0.893, 0.919]$
(Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}). The machinery itself is verified sound: in-sample calibration
is exact ($0.9005 = 1801/2000$) and the oracle covers at nominal. The seed-1 shortfall was
therefore investigated rather than accepted: coverage is invariant to the score
randomization (spread $<0.0005$ over 20 draws), calibration and test scores are
indistinguishable (KS $p = 0.51$), and swapping the two splits \emph{over}-covers by the
mirror amount ($0.917$). A permutation test puts the shortfall at $p = 0.0045$ under
exchangeability: a genuine $\sim$1-in-220 fluctuation of this (model, calibration, test)
triple, not a defect. With that caveat, the tool supports evidence-grade language
(``the source is in this region with $90\%$ confidence''), and confidently-wrong outputs
are eliminated at approximately the stated rate.

\subsection{H2: Sharpness (exact-posterior audit)}
\label{sec:h2}

At matched verified $90\%$ coverage, the per-scenario Spearman correlation between learned
and exact areas is $0.848$ --- the model ranks scenario difficulty correctly --- but the
inefficiency factor is $47.6$ (mean) / $5.6$ (median): at the median the learned regions
are $5.6\times$ larger than the data physically permit, and the mean is dominated by
scenarios where the exact posterior is razor-sharp (area $\sim 10^{-3}$) while the learned
heatmap saturates near $0.4$ of the domain (Figure~\ref{fig:audit}, left). The
preregistered reading applies: \emph{the network, not the data, is the bottleneck}. Large
\emph{exact} regions indicate the sensor network is the limiting factor (a placement
signal); large \emph{ratios}, as found here, indicate headroom for better amortized
inference --- which Sec.~\ref{sec:distill} realizes.

\subsection{H3: Dose--response}
\label{sec:h3}

Contraction-slope ratios (learned $\div$ exact) are $0.47$ (Knob 1, sensor count) and
$0.49$ (Knob 3, noise); per-knob Spearman agreements are $0.81$, $0.88$, $0.75$
(per-scenario medians). For the geometry pairs (Knob 2), the median confined:spread area
ratio is $1.20$ (learned) vs.\ $1.58$ (exact) --- means $2.4$ vs.\ $177.7$, the latter
dominated by a few confined layouts whose exact posterior explodes --- and both methods
widen under confinement in exactly $64\%$ of pairs. The learned tool reacts in the
direction the physics dictates on every knob --- tightening as sensors are added, widening
under ambiguous geometry --- but at roughly \emph{half} the oracle's magnitude, consistent
with H2: the regions are over-wide precisely where data are most informative
(Figure~\ref{fig:dose}). \textbf{D1:} region area and MAP error are strongly associated
(Figure~\ref{fig:audit}, right).

\subsection{The physics-distilled localizer: the audit certifies the improvement}
\label{sec:distill}

H2's diagnosis --- the network, not the data, is the bottleneck --- is testable, and the
distillation recipe of Sec.~\ref{sec:distillmethod} tests it with everything but the
training signal held fixed. Results (Table~\ref{tab:headline}; magenta in
Figures~\ref{fig:audit}--\ref{fig:dose}): coverage $0.899$ $[0.884, 0.911]$; median
region area $0.068 \to 0.053$; inefficiency median $5.6\times \to 3.9\times$ (mean
$47.6 \to 28.9$); H2 Spearman $0.848 \to 0.893$; JSD to the exact posterior
$0.56 \to 0.50$; median MAP error $0.103 \to 0.094$. The dose--response sharpens most:
the sensor-count slope ratio rises from $0.47$ to $0.87$ --- the distilled model tightens
at $87\%$ of the oracle's rate when a sensor is added --- and geometry agreement reaches
$0.90$ (noise: $0.54$). The gains land exactly where the audit located the deficiency ---
uncertainty \emph{shape} --- and barely move point accuracy, which was never the problem.
Interpretation: the benchmark's shipped posteriors double as a training resource that
closes roughly a third of the sharpness gap and most of the sensor-response gap with no
capacity or data change; the remaining $\sim 3.9\times$ cleanly quantifies what
amortization itself leaves on the table at this scale.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Broader Impact}

\paragraph{What the results mean.} The benchmark's exact posteriors earn their storage
twice. As an \emph{audit reference}: coverage alone would have certified the baseline
(after conformal calibration) while hiding a $5.6\times$ gap to the physical information
limit and a blunted response to added sensors --- only the exact-posterior comparison
exposes both, and conformal calibration meanwhile absorbs even the oracle's own
discretization overconfidence (Appendix~\ref{app:refine}). As a \emph{training signal}:
the same posteriors, distilled with tempering into the same model, recover a third of the
sharpness gap and most of the dose--response gap --- so the audit's diagnosis (``the
network is the bottleneck'') is not just descriptive but actionable, and the residual
$3.9\times$ isolates what amortization itself costs. We frame the benchmark as a
\emph{neutral screening instrument} for uncertainty claims: failing the audit is
disqualifying, passing is necessary but not sufficient --- and the instrument demonstrably
detects failure, certifies improvement, and quantifies remaining headroom.

\paragraph{Implementation lessons (portable).} (1) The mass-above conformal score of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} silently loses its guarantee in floating point on sharp heatmaps;
compute in tail space. (2) Fixed-node quadrature cannot marginalize a rate likelihood
whose peak width varies by orders of magnitude across hypotheses; place nodes
peak-adaptively. (3) At $10^4$ training scenarios the specified architecture memorizes
unless the problem's exact symmetry group is injected; and near-delta distillation targets
fail where mildly tempered ones succeed.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The study is synthetic, two-dimensional, single stationary source,
constant and known wind and diffusivity, Gaussian noise of known level, no obstacles,
turbulence, or background; the guarantee is marginal and in-distribution. \emph{Real sites
violate every one of these.} The societal claim is strictly that the reliability machinery
works and is worth transferring. We claim no new conformal algorithm, no new architecture,
no coverage under shift, no field deployability, no abstention (though D1 supports it as
future work). Regulation is cited purely as motivation. \textbf{Future work:} robust/weighted
conformal for misspecified meteorology; a Fisher-information localizability score
[Bangerth et al., 2022] with abstention; sim-to-real recalibration on controlled-release
data (METEC-style).

\section{Conclusion}

We presented a benchmark for uncertainty-aware source localization built around a single
asset --- the exact Bayesian posterior of every scenario, shipped with the data --- and
showed that this asset both audits and trains. As an audit, it tests any localizer three
ways (finite-sample coverage, sharpness against the physical information limit, causal
dose--response) and returned a differentiated, preregistered verdict on a standard
baseline: calibrated, not sharp, directionally stable. As a training signal, the same
posteriors --- distilled with tempering into the same model on the same data --- sharpen
regions from $5.6\times$ to $3.9\times$ the physical limit and nearly restore
oracle-proportional response to added sensors, certifying the audit's diagnosis and
isolating the residual cost of amortization. The implementation lessons on conformal
scores, quadrature, symmetry, and teacher tempering transfer well beyond pollution
localization.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}

A single configuration file drives every stage; a single script
(\texttt{scripts/run\_all.sh}) regenerates the study from a clean checkout. Scenario
regeneration is bit-identical (SHA-256 verified). We release the generator, frozen
scenario IDs and seeds, configs, exact per-scenario posteriors for the train, validation,
calibration, and test splits, dose--response sets, model checkpoints, and a ready-to-run
subset. Total compute: $\approx 1.6$ GPU-hours on one NVIDIA L40S ($0.011$ GPU-h per
baseline seed; $\approx 0.1$ GPU-h for all distillation variants) and 300 MB of data ---
far under the preregistered 12--20 GPU-hour / 10 GB budget.

\section{Quadrature and Validation Details}
\label{app:quadrature}

Age integral: 64-node Gauss--Legendre under $s = t\,e^{-\tau}$, $\tau_{\max} = 12$ (tuned:
at $\tau_{\max} = 40$ the convergence gate fails at $2\times10^{-4}$; the truncated tail
contributes $\lesssim 10^{-6}$ relative even for a sensor at the source). Gates: 64- vs
128-node median relative error $3.6\times10^{-11}$ (gate $<10^{-6}$; the 90th percentile,
$6.9\times10^{-4}$, concentrates on probes many orders of magnitude below the noise floor;
generation and oracle share the same operator and remain exactly self-consistent).
Zero-wind closed form (exponential integrals): median relative error $1.2\times10^{-13}$.
Second-order finite-difference cross-check (central differences, Heun, CFL-stable,
$h = 1/768$, enlarged Dirichlet domain), 20 random scenarios: maximum $0.50\%$ relative
$L_2$ at sensors, median $0.06\%$, over the 17 scenarios with signal; the 3 no-signal
scenarios (reference RMS $10^{-18}$--$10^{-9}$) agree in absolute RMS to
$\leq 8.8\times10^{-11}$ (gated at $10^{-4}$, $1\%$ of the smallest noise std, since
relative error is ill-posed at zero signal). Symmetry equivariance: $1.7\times10^{-13}$;
linearity: $2.7\times10^{-16}$.

\section{Exact-Posterior Sanity Checks}
\label{app:sanity}

Normalization within $3.4\times10^{-13}$ (gate $10^{-6}$). Mode $\to$ true cell as
$\sigma \to 0$: 24/24, with the check made well-posed by snapping sanity sources to cell
centers (the candidate set \emph{is} the centers), conditioning on identifiability (a
sensor must see the plume above the smallest noise floor; all-upwind layouts differ by
$\sim10^{-30}$ between adjacent cells), and $\sigma = 10^{-6}$. Confined layouts yield
visibly broad posteriors ($90\%$ HPD area $0.48$ vs.\ $0.064$ well-spread).
Conformal-on-exact-posterior: $0.907$ $[0.893, 0.919]$. Area contracts with $q$ for
$87.5\%$ of 24 cell-centered scenarios (mean $\Delta = -0.005$) and grows with $\sigma$
for $100\%$ (mean $+0.024$); per-realization $q$-monotonicity is not guaranteed at the
rate-prior edges, where the prior truncates the location--rate confusion set
asymmetrically. Brute-force marginalization cross-check: $\leq 9.4\times10^{-7}$
(gate $10^{-6}$).

\section{Grid-Refinement Check}
\label{app:refine}

Recomputing exact posteriors on a $96 \times 96$ grid and re-running the identical
conformal procedure on the \emph{full} test split changes coverage by $-0.007$ ($0.9005$
vs.\ $0.9070$) and median area by $-0.006$ (finer cells resolve sharper regions),
confirming the $64 \times 64$ grid is adequate. (We use the full split rather than the
preregistered 100 scenarios: 100-scenario windows have coverage $0.79$--$0.97$ and cannot
resolve the effect.) Normalization error at $96^2$: $1.4\times10^{-13}$.
