\begin{abstract}
Rapid repair of methane super-emitters is among the highest-leverage near-term climate
actions, and satellite and sensor programs now detect far more releases than are acted on:
attributing a detection to a specific source, with \emph{trustworthy} uncertainty, is the
bottleneck. A fast neural localizer can output a source heatmap over a facility, and split
conformal prediction can turn it into a region that provably contains the true source
$90\%$ of the time --- but coverage alone does not say whether that region is
\emph{actionably small}. We study this gap on CH4-500, a methane-configured testbed
(500 m site, steady-state Gaussian plume with Pasquill--Gifford stability classes,
$10$--$500$ kg/h releases spanning the super-emitter threshold, ppm-level sensors) whose
tractable physics lets us compute the exact Bayesian posterior of every scenario. These
exact posteriors serve twice --- as a dense training signal and as the reference that
measures how close a learned localizer comes to the physical information limit. Standard
location-label training yields a calibrated localizer whose $90\%$ regions are, on
identifiable scenarios, a median $32\times$ larger than the data permit. Distilling
mildly tempered exact posteriors into the \emph{identical} network --- changing only the
training target --- cuts this to $8.5\times$, sharper on $94\%$ of scenarios
(paired $p < 10^{-12}$), while barely changing point accuracy; raw untempered targets
fail to optimize. A stratified audit shows the gap is model-limited only where sensors
are dense, and that over half of random sparse layouts are physically un-localizable ---
a network-design finding. The simulator, exact posteriors, and full pipeline are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Methane is responsible for roughly a third of observed warming to date, and because it
clears the atmosphere in about a decade, cutting it slows warming on human timescales;
oil-and-gas ``super-emitters'' --- point releases of $\geq$100 kg/h --- are a small,
individually fixable share of the total, making their rapid repair one of the
highest-leverage climate actions available. Detection is increasingly solved: the UNEP
Methane Alert and Response System issued $\sim$1,200 satellite notifications in two years,
of which roughly $1\%$ drew a documented response [UNEP, 2024]. A principal obstacle is
\emph{attribution}: turning a coarse detection into a trustworthy statement about
\emph{which} facility or equipment is leaking. Regulation now makes this concrete --- under
the U.S.\ Super-Emitter Program obligations attach to operators within 50 m of a reported
location [U.S. EPA, 2024], and EU Regulation 2024/1787 mandates energy-sector methane MRV
[European Union, 2024] --- so an attribution's \emph{uncertainty} is itself operationally
and legally material.

A learned localizer trained on dispersion simulations can map sparse sensor readings to a
source heatmap in milliseconds, and split conformal prediction can wrap that heatmap in a
region with a finite-sample coverage guarantee. This is necessary but not sufficient.
Coverage is a statement about \emph{frequency}, not \emph{efficiency}: a $90\%$ region
covering half the site is calibrated and useless, sending crews to search far more area
than the readings justify. What one wants is a region as small as the physics permits ---
and, crucially, a way to know whether a given localizer achieves that.

\textbf{Our observation is that in simulation-based training the exact Bayesian posterior
of every scenario can be computed offline and used two ways at once: as the reference
that measures how close a localizer comes to the physical information limit, and as a
dense training signal that closes much of the gap.} Standard training teaches only the
realized source (``the leak was here''), never the shape of the uncertainty --- which
alternative locations fit, when the posterior is a ridge or multimodal, how much a sensor
tightens it. The exact posterior teaches exactly that. We study this on a
methane-configured testbed and find: a calibrated baseline whose regions are a median
$32\times$ too large on identifiable scenarios; a physics-distilled variant --- same
network, same data, only the target changed --- that cuts this to $8.5\times$ with point
accuracy unchanged; a tempering requirement (raw posterior targets fail); and a stratified
picture that turns the audit into sensor-network guidance.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)} \emph{Physics-posterior distillation}: training an amortized localizer against
exact simulator posteriors as soft targets improves calibrated-region sharpness
substantially (identifiable-scenario inefficiency $32\times \to 8.5\times$, noise-response
ratio $0.001 \to 0.72$) at no inference cost, with point accuracy nearly unchanged ---
isolating the deficiency as posterior approximation, not localization. Mild teacher
tempering is a necessary ingredient.
\textbf{(2)} \emph{An exact-posterior audit}: conformal calibration equalizes coverage
across models so region size becomes a pure sharpness measurement against the physical
limit; a dose--response protocol tests whether uncertainty reacts to sensors, geometry,
and noise as the physics dictates; stratification localizes the amortization gap.
\textbf{(3)} \emph{A released methane-configured benchmark} (CH4-500) shipping exact
per-scenario posteriors --- the enabling artifact for both the supervision and the
measurement.

\paragraph{Scope.} CH4-500 is a controlled testbed, not a deployment model: steady-state
2-D-sensed Gaussian plume, flat terrain, known stability, fixed release height. The
idealization is deliberate --- it is what makes the exact posterior computable, so the
trust machinery can be validated against ground truth. The path to the field runs through
recalibration on controlled-release data (e.g., METEC ranges); we claim the reliability
layer, not field readiness.

\section{The CH4-500 Testbed}
\label{sec:testbed}

\paragraph{Physics.} A steady-state Gaussian plume on a $500 \times 500$ m site: a source
at height $H = 3$ m releasing $Q$ kg/h into wind $\boldsymbol{u}$ under Pasquill--Gifford
stability class $s \in \{$A$,\dots,$F$\}$ produces, at a $z_R = 2$ m sensor, an enhancement
\begin{equation}
C(\boldsymbol{x}) = \frac{Q}{2\pi\, U\, \sigma_y(x')\, \sigma_z(x')}
\exp\!\Big(\!-\tfrac{y'^2}{2\sigma_y^2}\Big)
\Big[e^{-\frac{(z_R-H)^2}{2\sigma_z^2}} + e^{-\frac{(z_R+H)^2}{2\sigma_z^2}}\Big],
\label{eq:plume}
\end{equation}
with $(x', y')$ the down/crosswind coordinates and $\sigma_{y,z}$ the Briggs open-country
dispersion curves for class $s$ (ground reflection is the second exponential). The response
is linear in $Q$ and closed-form --- no quadrature --- so the exact-posterior machinery
below applies directly. Physical gates pass: exact crosswind mirror symmetry
($4\times10^{-14}$), numerical mass-flux conservation $\iint U C\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}z
= Q$ ($4\times10^{-6}$), and $80$ ppm at $100$ m downwind of a $100$ kg/h release in class
D (literature-plausible).

\paragraph{Scenarios.} Per scenario (Table~\ref{tab:prior}, Figure~\ref{fig:gallery}):
source location uniform on the site, $Q \sim$ LogU$(10, 500)$ kg/h, wind $1$--$8$ m/s in a
uniform direction, stability class uniform over A--F, $N \sim$U$\{4,\dots,12\}$ sensor
masts at uniform positions, noise $\sigma \sim$LogU$(0.2, 3)$ ppm, and Bernoulli reading
dropout. Each mast reports 30 time-averaged readings. Splits (10k/1k/2k/2k
train/val/calib/test) are by complete scenario; a leakage audit confirms zero overlap.

\paragraph{Exact posteriors.} For candidate cell $c$ of a $64\times64$ grid with unit-rate
response $\boldsymbol{g}_c$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:plume}, the Gaussian likelihood
$p(\boldsymbol{y}\mid c, Q) = \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{y}; Q\boldsymbol{g}_c, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})$ is
marginalized over a LogUniform rate prior and normalized over cells to give
$p(c\mid\boldsymbol{y})$ --- exact for this simulator and grid. Marginalization uses a
peak-aware composite quadrature (the naive fixed rule errs by many nats when the
rate-likelihood peak is narrow); normalization holds to $10^{-13}$, and the posterior mode
recovers the true cell as $\sigma \to 0$ on identifiable scenarios (18/24; the rest are
exact along-wind ridge degeneracies). Posteriors are computed for all splits: train/val
become teachers (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}), calib/test the audit reference
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Localizer (fixed across all models).} A permutation-invariant DeepSets network
(4.87M params): each retained reading is tokenized as
$[\mathrm{Fourier}(x_i), \mathrm{Fourier}(y_i), \mathrm{Fourier}(t_k),
\operatorname{asinh}(y_{ik}/\sigma)]$, embedded by a shared MLP, pooled by masked mean and
max, concatenated with a meteorology-context MLP over $(\boldsymbol{u}, s, \sigma, N)$, and
decoded to a softmax over the grid. Inference is $0.48$ ms/scenario. Training uses AdamW,
cosine schedule, batch 256, early stopping. Each batch is augmented by a random D4 symmetry
of the square applied jointly to sensors, wind, and source --- an exact symmetry under
which the prior is invariant; without it this network memorizes the training set with zero
generalization (Figure~\ref{fig:memo}).

\paragraph{M0: point-label supervision.} The standard target: cross-entropy to a smoothed
one-hot bump at the realized source. This teaches location, not uncertainty shape.

\paragraph{M1: physics-posterior distillation.} We replace the target with the scenario's
exact posterior (Eq.~\eqref{eq:plume} $\to$ likelihood $\to$ posterior), tempered by an
isotropic Gaussian of $0.75$ cells,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_c \big[G_{0.75} * p(\cdot\mid\boldsymbol{y})\big](c)\,
\log p_\theta(c\mid\boldsymbol{y}),
\label{eq:distill}
\end{equation}
with the teacher grid permuted by the same D4 element as the inputs, and early stopping on
validation teacher cross-entropy. Everything else --- architecture, data, optimizer,
inference cost --- is identical to M0. Tempering is necessary: with the raw teacher,
identifiable-scenario inefficiency is $11.3\times$ vs.\ M1's $8.5\times$ and training is
unstable (Figure~\ref{fig:targets}); near-delta targets give gradients dominated by cells
the student cannot yet match.

\paragraph{Conformal regionization (the measurement control).} Every model, and the exact
posterior itself, is wrapped by the identical randomized highest-density-mass split
conformal procedure: a nonconformity score on 2000 calibration scenarios, a threshold at
the $\lceil(n{+}1)(1{-}\alpha)\rceil$-th value, regions filled by descending probability.
Under exchangeability each wrapped model has $\Pr(c^\star \in \mathcal{C}_\alpha) \geq
1-\alpha$. This equalizes coverage across models, so region-size differences isolate
sharpness. (One numerical note: the standard mass-above score saturates in floating point
on sharp maps and silently loses coverage; we compute in complement space, which is
exact-arithmetic-equivalent.)

\paragraph{Audit metrics.} With learned and exact regions both at verified $90\%$ coverage,
the per-scenario area ratio (learned/exact) is the \emph{inefficiency factor}; $1$ means
the localizer extracts all the information the readings contain. A dose--response protocol
adds/removes sensors, contrasts geometries, and sweeps noise on constructed sites, and
reports learned-vs-exact area-response slopes. Because the plume is thin and steady, many
random sparse layouts are weakly identifiable, so we report inefficiency stratified by
oracle difficulty rather than a single overall median.

\subsection{Point-label supervision is calibrated but not actionable}
\label{sec:r-m0}

Conformal M0 attains near-nominal coverage ($0.90$, verified even on top of the
discretized oracle; Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}) --- and draws the same over-broad region
almost regardless of how much the readings actually constrain the source
(Figure~\ref{fig:zoo}, middle row). On identifiable scenarios (oracle region $<10\%$ of the
site) its regions are a median $32.5\times$ larger than the exact posterior permits, and
its area barely responds to noise (slope ratio $0.001$). Point accuracy is unremarkable
but fine (median MAP error $162$--$200$ m, near the oracle's $164$ m and better than the
$205$ m peak-sensor heuristic): what point labels fail to teach is not \emph{where}, but
\emph{how unsure} (Figure~\ref{fig:audit}).

  \centering

  \caption{\textbf{Posterior shape across identifiability.} Columns: five test scenarios
  ordered by oracle HPD-90 area. Rows: exact posterior, M0, M1 (star = true source). The
  exact posterior collapses to sharp along-wind ridges when identifiable; M0 draws a
  similar broad blob regardless; M1 tracks the physical shape.}
  \label{fig:zoo}

\subsection{Physics-posterior distillation closes most of the gap}
\label{sec:r-m1}

Changing only the training target (M1) cuts identifiable-scenario inefficiency from
$32.5\times$ to $8.5\times$ (bootstrap $95\%$ CIs $[28.7, 38.2]$ vs.\ $[7.5, 11.1]$;
sharper on $94\%$ of scenarios; paired Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-12}$), lowers JSD to the exact
posterior from $0.377$ to $0.235$, and improves median MAP error slightly ($200 \to 162$
m) --- all at unchanged inference cost (Table~\ref{tab:headline}, Figure~\ref{fig:audit}).
The improvement is concentrated exactly where the data are informative
(Figure~\ref{fig:zoo}) and is seed-stable with no metric overlap (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}).
The raw-teacher failure and the memorization-without-augmentation collapse
(Figure~\ref{fig:memo}) are the two ingredients that make the physics signal usable.

\subsection{Dose--response: distillation restores physically correct behavior}
\label{sec:r-dose}

On constructed sites we vary information and measure how region area responds
(Figure~\ref{fig:dose}, \ref{fig:knobs}). \textbf{Sensor masts:} M1's area contracts as
masts are added at $0.75\times$ the oracle rate with rank agreement $0.92$, while M0
scarcely responds (slope ratio near zero, negative rank agreement). \textbf{Noise:} M1
tracks the oracle's noise response (slope ratio $0.72$, agreement $0.71$) where M0 is flat
($0.001$). \textbf{Geometry:} M1 agrees with the oracle on which confined layouts widen the
region (agreement $0.86$ vs.\ M0's $0.27$). In each case the point-label model reacts to
information far more weakly than the physics dictates, and the physics target largely
repairs it.

\subsection{Where the gap lives, and a network-design finding}
\label{sec:r-strat}

Stratifying the audit (Figure~\ref{fig:stratified}) shows the gap is confined to
information-rich scenarios: with sparse masts (4--6) or in weakly identifiable geometries
both models sit near the physical limit ($\approx\!1\times$) --- the sensor network, not
the model, binds --- while the $32\times \to 8.5\times$ improvement is entirely in the
dense, identifiable regime. Two consequences. First, an investment rule: below
$\sim$7 masts, add sensors; above, improve the model. Second, and more striking:
\emph{over half} ($57\%$) of scenarios with randomly placed masts are weakly identifiable
(oracle region $\geq 50\%$ of the site), because thin steady plumes simply miss randomly
placed sensors --- a quantitative argument that methane monitoring networks require
deliberate placement, not density alone. Distillation also makes conditional coverage more
uniform across strata ($0.906$--$0.928$ vs.\ M0's $0.862$--$0.913$).

  decile --- the gap lives where the oracle is sharp. Right: conditional coverage by
  stratum; distillation is sharper and more uniformly calibrated.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{The gap is data-limited; distillation is worth extra data}
\label{sec:r-scale}

Retraining at $\{2.5, 10, 40\}$k scenarios (Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}) shows
identifiable-scenario inefficiency falling with data for both supervisions and \emph{not}
plateauing by 40k --- so at this architecture the residual gap is predominantly a training
budget limit, not a capacity floor. Distillation stays ahead throughout (M1@10k
$\approx$ M0@40k: $8.5\times$ vs.\ $8.6\times$), i.e.\ the physics target buys roughly the
sharpness of a $4\times$ larger point-labeled set at this range --- a direct data-efficiency
statement for standing up attribution models on new facility types.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Operational reading.} At facility scale the median identifiable-scenario region
shrinks under distillation while coverage holds, and region area is a direct proxy for
ground-survey effort, so sharper regions mean fewer candidate sites per satellite alert and
faster repair; region area also predicts localization error (Figure~\ref{fig:audit}),
supporting an abstention rule (``flag, don't dispatch, when the region is large''). The
audit doubles as procurement infrastructure: a regulator can screen any vendor localizer
for calibrated \emph{and} sharp attribution against exact references. These are readings of
a controlled testbed, offered as design guidance, not deployment claims.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.} Amortized neural posterior estimation and conformal UQ
are standard [Angelopoulos and Bates, 2021; Cabezas et al., 2025]; Distill-Belief [Shi et
al., 2026] distills a Bayes-correct particle-filter teacher for source localization. We
distill \emph{exact} grid posteriors, audit sharpness against the same posteriors at matched
coverage, and use conformal prediction as a measurement control rather than a contribution.
Gaussian-plume inversion is the standard methane STE model [Newman et al., 2024]; we add
the amortized-uncertainty audit and the physics-derived training signal.

\paragraph{Limitations.} CH4-500 is idealized: steady-state 2-D-sensed plume, flat terrain,
known stability class, fixed and known release height, Gaussian noise, no background or
obstacles; ``exact'' is exact for this simulator, not the real atmosphere. Coverage is
marginal and in-distribution. Distillation requires a tractable per-scenario posterior at
training time (automatic here; an approximation problem in richer simulators). We study one
architecture. The path to deployment runs through controlled-release recalibration and
integration with real monitoring networks --- future work, not claimed here.

\section{Conclusion}

Trustworthy methane attribution, not detection, gates methane response. Using the physics
simulator behind an attribution tool to compute exact per-scenario posteriors, we show
those posteriors can both audit a learned localizer against the physical information limit
and, as a training signal, close most of the gap between a calibrated-but-vague baseline and
that limit --- cutting identifiable-scenario region inefficiency from $32\times$ to
$8.5\times$ at no inference cost, with a stratified audit that tells monitoring programs
whether to invest in sensors or software. The methane-configured benchmark and its exact
posteriors are released.

\section*{Reproducibility}
One config drives every stage; scenario regeneration is bit-identical (SHA-256 verified).
We release the plume simulator, frozen scenarios, exact per-scenario posteriors for all
splits, dose--response sets, checkpoints, and the audit pipeline. Total compute:
$\approx 3$ GPU-hours on one L40S.
