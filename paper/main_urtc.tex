\title{Reaching the Actionable Radius: Physics-Guided Posterior Distillation
for Methane Source Attribution}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Eric Liang}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Department of Computer Science, Boston University\\
eric.jinchen.liang@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present a simple, drop-in training recipe that brings calibrated
machine-learning attribution of methane super-emitters under the U.S.~EPA's
50\,m actionability radius --- the precision at which a detection becomes a
repair. On a methane-configured testbed whose closed-form physics makes the
\emph{exact} Bayesian posterior computable for every scenario, we first
contribute an audit that measures, in meters, how much of the physically
available information a calibrated localizer delivers: a conformal-wrapped
deep localizer with a verified 90\% coverage guarantee achieves a 94\,m median
search radius where the data support 7\,m. Guided by a new one-run diagnostic
--- conformalizing the distillation \emph{teacher} itself --- we localize the
opportunity: the missing ingredient is a classical computation (per-cell
matched filtering) that pooled set encoders cannot represent, rather than
better supervision or a more modern density estimator. Our recipe supplies it:
two closed-form matched-filter maps as input channels, a zero-initialized
residual head (0.2\% extra parameters), and distillation from the tempered
exact posterior. Across three architecture families and nine seed-runs, every
model reaches 43.6--49.3\,m at unchanged coverage and millisecond latency,
outperforming a fairness-hardened flow-based neural posterior estimator
(55.5--71.2\,m). Under realistic wind-measurement error, our distilled
students retain calibration where exact-physics inversion cannot (0.89--0.91
vs.\ 0.70 coverage), and uncertainty-aware input maps recover a further
12\,m --- evidence that learned, physics-guided attribution is not only
sharper but more robust than the physics alone.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
methane monitoring, conformal prediction, simulation-based inference,

\section{Introduction}
Methane delivers roughly $80\times$ the 20-year warming potency of CO$_2$ and
decays within about a decade, making rapid repair of oil-and-gas
\emph{super-emitters} one of the highest-leverage near-term climate actions.
Detection has advanced rapidly --- UNEP's satellite alert system issued over a
thousand notifications in its first two years \cite{unep} --- and the
remaining step is \emph{attribution}: converting a detection into a source
location trustworthy enough to dispatch a crew. Regulation makes the target
concrete: the EPA Super-Emitter Program operationalizes actionability as a
50\,m radius \cite{epa}, with analogous source-level duties in the EU
\cite{eu}. Continuous monitoring with sparse networks of fixed point sensors
is the tier of the monitoring stack where this can happen: a handful of cheap
masts around a facility, always on, catching the plume as it sweeps across
them under naturally meandering wind.

Machine learning supplies the speed, and conformal prediction \cite{conformal}
supplies a guarantee: a neural localizer maps readings to a probability map,
and a split-conformal wrapper converts it into a region that provably contains
the source 90\% of the time. \textbf{The gap} is that coverage guarantees
frequency, not usefulness. Two questions had no quantitative answer: (i)~how
close is a calibrated region to the \emph{smallest} region the sensor data
physically support, and (ii)~if there is a gap, what mechanism causes it and
what closes it? Existing diagnostics for amortized inference check whether a
posterior is well-calibrated \cite{sbc}, not how far it is from the exact
posterior at matched calibration --- a question that requires exact posteriors
to answer, and that determines whether ML monitoring meets the regulatory bar.

\textbf{Contributions.} We answer both questions constructively:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1 --- An exact-posterior audit.} A methane testbed (CH4-T)
whose closed-form physics makes the true Bayesian posterior of every scenario
computable, so the efficiency of any calibrated localizer can be measured in
meters against the information limit (7\,m) and the regulatory radius (50\,m).
\item \textbf{C2 --- A diagnosis, by elimination, with a reusable tool.}
Conformalizing the distillation \emph{teacher} decomposes the gap into a
supervision ceiling (11.7\,m) versus student approximation (94\,m), and
controlled tests of four rival explanations identify the true bottleneck: a
classical computation the encoder cannot represent.
\item \textbf{C3 --- A recipe that reaches the bar.} Physics-guided posterior
distillation --- matched-filter input maps, a zero-initialized residual head,
and a tempered exact-posterior teacher --- brings three different architecture
families to 43.6--49.3\,m at unchanged coverage and latency, and remains
calibrated under wind-measurement error that defeats exact-physics inversion.
\end{itemize}
The components are deliberately classical --- matched filters are 1940s
detection theory; distillation and conformal prediction are standard --- and
we verify empirically that this is a strength: the composition outperforms the
current flow-based state of the art at identical information.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Conformal prediction} provides distribution-free coverage
\cite{conformal}; efficiency (region size) is recognized as the open half of
the problem. We contribute the first audit we are aware of that scores
conformal efficiency against the \emph{exact} Bayesian limit at matched,
verified coverage. \textbf{Simulation-based inference} amortizes posteriors
with neural density estimators \cite{npe,nsf}, benchmarked at simulation
budgets matching ours \cite{sbibm}; its standard diagnostics test calibration
\cite{sbc} but cannot measure distance from the exact posterior --- our audit
asks precisely that, and our flow-based baseline places the current SBI
standard \emph{inside} the comparison. \textbf{Source-term estimation}
inverts dispersion models via optimization or MCMC at minutes-to-hours per
event \cite{newman}; deep localizers achieve millisecond inference but have
lacked instruments to certify how much information they extract.
\textbf{Physics-guided ML and distillation} inject domain structure through
features, losses, or teachers \cite{hinton}; our teacher is an exact Bayesian
oracle rather than a larger network (compressing inference cost, not
parameters), and our input maps are the forward model's sufficient
statistics. \textbf{Controlled-release evaluation} (METEC) scores continuous
monitoring systems on detection, quantification, and equipment-scale
localization \cite{metec}; our testbed mirrors that task structure at
super-emitter rates with exact ground truth, complementing --- not replacing
--- field validation.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Testbed and exact-posterior audit (C1)}
\textbf{CH4-T} places a source of unknown location and rate
(10--500\,kg\,h$^{-1}$, log-uniform, bracketing the 100\,kg\,h$^{-1}$
super-emitter threshold) on a $500{\times}500$\,m site sensed by 4--12 masts
(ppm readings, 0.2--3\,ppm noise, dropout). The plume is a steady Gaussian
under known Pasquill--Gifford stability with Briggs open-country dispersion
(coefficients verified against published tables; mass flux conserved to
$4{\times}10^{-6}$), driven quasi-statically by a known fluctuating wind
record (1--8\,m\,s$^{-1}$, AR(1) meander; steps read as 1-min averages, a
30-min window). The veering wind sweeps the plume across the masts;
this time-of-arrival information makes 94\% of scenarios well-identifiable
(vs.\ 43\% under constant wind).

Because the response is closed-form and linear in rate, the exact posterior
over a $64{\times}64$ grid is computable per scenario (adaptive rate
quadrature, float64, normalization error $\sim$$10^{-12}$). A blocking gate
requires conformal prediction applied to the exact posterior itself to
self-calibrate (0.95 observed) --- a gate that caught two implementation bugs
during development. All models share identical data (10k/1k/2k/2k
train/val/calib/test, the modal SBI budget \cite{sbibm}), identical D4
symmetry augmentation, and an identical randomized-HPD split-conformal
wrapper at verified 90\% coverage in numerically safe tail-mass form. Region
sizes are reported as equal-area circle radii.

\subsection{Locating the opportunity (C2)}
A DeepSets localizer trained on one-hot labels is calibrated (coverage 0.89)
at a 94\,m median radius --- the information for 7\,m is in the readings,
waiting to be extracted. Distilling the exact posterior helps (74\,m) and
reveals structure: the raw near-delta posterior must be tempered by a
0.75-cell blur to be learnable (raw: 134\,m). Our diagnostic then asks how
much the tempered teacher itself supports: running the same conformal audit
\emph{on the teacher} yields 11.7\,m --- so supervision content permits a
$6\times$ sharper student, and the remaining gap is approximation. Controlled
tests eliminate the remaining suspects: sharper targets (anneal: 87\,m), a
geometry-aware Sinkhorn transport loss (243\,m; implementation-scoped), and
the current SBI-standard flow head --- given a $2.5\times$ budget, a widened
head, and full-band conditioning after adversarial review of our own
implementation --- improves the labels-only baseline to 62.8--71.2\,m, and we
verify its spline head can express 4.4\,m regions, so its remaining blur is
not head-structural. What survives elimination: localization requires
correlating each of 4{,}096 candidate cells' space--time signature against
all readings --- \emph{per-cell matched filtering} --- which a pooled set
representation cannot carry. The opportunity is to supply that computation.

\subsection{The recipe (C3)}
For any base localizer (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}): \textbf{(1)}~compute per
cell $c$ the sufficient statistics $a_c{=}\sum g_c y$, $b_c{=}\sum g_c^2$ of
the linear-Gaussian model ($g_c$ the unit-rate response) and feed two
$64{\times}64$ channels, the matched-filter statistic
$z_c{=}a_c/(\sigma\sqrt{b_c})$ and $\log b_c$ --- milliseconds, closed form,
computed from the network's own inputs (no new information, only a
computation); \textbf{(2)}~add a 3-layer zero-initialized conv head
($\sim$10k parameters, 0.2\% of the base) summed with the localizer's logits,
making every comparison controlled by construction; \textbf{(3)}~train with
cross-entropy to the tempered exact posterior --- the maps supply evidence,
the teacher teaches how to weigh evidence into calibrated probability;
\textbf{(4)}~wrap with unchanged split conformal.

\textbf{Why amortize when the teacher exists?} On this testbed the teacher is
the measuring instrument, tractable by construction. In deployment,
realistic dispersion has no closed form and simulator inversion costs hours
per event \cite{newman} where monitoring needs seconds --- and, as
\S\ref{sec:robust} shows, the distilled student holds calibration under
sensor-measurement error that exact inversion does not survive. Every
simulated training set already contains its own uncertainty labels; this
recipe is how to extract them.

Every ingredient improves every architecture, and all nine full-recipe
seed-runs land under the 50\,m bar (Table~\ref{tab:main};
Fig.~\ref{fig:results}A). Paired Wilcoxon on per-scenario log region sizes:
$p<10^{-200}$. Two further findings strengthen the result. First, the spread
across architectures collapses (94-vs-56 without the recipe; 49-vs-44 with
it): supervision and inputs, not encoder design, were the binding
constraint, so gains are available to whatever architecture a practitioner
already has. Second, point accuracy is essentially unchanged --- the recipe
improves the \emph{shape of uncertainty}, which is exactly what region size
measures and what dispatch decisions need.

The flow head is a genuine advance --- it outperforms plain distillation with
no physics at all --- and our levers transfer onto it monotonically
(Table~\ref{tab:flow}). The full recipe stays ahead: a convolutional head
reading the maps \emph{spatially} extracts more than the flow's parametric
conditioning at identical information. Our approach is thus compared
against, and complements, the current standard rather than being superseded
by it.

\subsection{Robust where exact physics is not}
\label{sec:robust}
\begin{table}[t]
\centering

speed): learned students retain calibration; modeling input uncertainty
recovers sharpness.}
\label{tab:wind}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Pipeline & Coverage & Radius \\
\midrule
Exact-physics oracle & 0.70 & 232\,m \\
Ours, deterministic maps & 0.91 & 94.7\,m \\
\textbf{Ours, ensemble maps} & 0.89 & \textbf{82.3\,m} \\
\bottomrule

Real anemometers err. Under a 10$^\circ$/10\% observation error, exact
inversion assigns numerically zero probability to the true source in 30\% of
scenarios, and no calibration procedure can repair a zero --- its conformal
coverage falls to 0.70 (Table~\ref{tab:wind}). The distilled student,
trained through the same noise, \emph{retains} its guarantee: a softmax never
declares a location impossible, and training widens uncertainty exactly where
wind error demands. Making the physics inputs uncertainty-aware ---
\emph{ensemble maps}, the mean and spread of the matched-filter maps over
eight draws from the wind-error model --- recovers 12\,m ($p{=}10^{-122}$).
Amortization thus delivers robustness the exact physics cannot: the student
outlives its own teacher, and modeling input uncertainty converts
measurement error from a failure mode into a quantified, budgeted cost.

\section{Limitations}
CH4-T is an instrument, not a deployment: single steady sources (no
zero-leak or multi-source scenarios), flat terrain without equipment wakes,
known stability class, quasi-static stepping, in-distribution marginal
coverage. Rates sit in the super-emitter regime, above the
$\leq$7\,kg\,h$^{-1}$ range of standard METEC continuous-monitoring tests
\cite{metec}; localization granularity matches what such facilities score.
The residual factor of $\sim$6 to the 7\,m limit is open headroom; the
transport-loss null is implementation-scoped; a wind-marginalized Bayes limit
under measurement error is future work (the marginalization factorizes over
timesteps; reliable quadrature requires bearing-adaptive panels). Field
claims await controlled-release validation --- and every comparison reported
here is exact regardless, because all models share one verified simulator:
fidelity bounds transfer, not the tables.

\section{Conclusions}
Three numbers organize this work: the physics allows 7\,m, regulators ask for
50\,m, and a standard calibrated localizer delivers 94\,m. We contribute the
instrument that measures this gap (C1), the diagnosis that locates its
mechanism --- a classical computation, not a learning deficiency (C2) ---
and a deliberately simple recipe that closes it on every architecture tested,
at unchanged cost, while remaining calibrated under the sensor error that
defeats exact inversion (C3). The broader message is constructive: simulated
training data already contain their own uncertainty labels, classical signal
processing already knows computations networks need not rediscover, and
combining the two turns a coverage guarantee into an actionable one. We hope
the evidence standard --- exact-posterior audits, blocking self-calibration
gates, one-change-at-a-time controls with documented nulls --- travels
furthest of all.
