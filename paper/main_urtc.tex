\title{Calibrated Is Not Actionable: Physics-Guided Posterior Distillation
for Methane Source Attribution}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Eric Liang}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Department of Computer Science, Boston University\\
eric.jinchen.liang@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Satellites now detect methane super-emitters routinely, yet only $\sim$1\% of
alerts lead to repairs: the bottleneck is \emph{attribution} --- naming a source
precisely enough to act, which U.S.~EPA rules quantify as a 50\,m radius. On a
methane testbed whose closed-form physics makes the \emph{exact} Bayesian
posterior computable for every scenario, we show that a conformal-wrapped deep
localizer with a verified 90\% coverage guarantee still yields a median search
radius of 94\,m --- honestly calibrated, but far above both the 50\,m bar and the
7\,m information limit the sensor data actually supports. We diagnose this gap
by running the same conformal audit on every pipeline stage, acquitting the
sensors (7\,m) and the distillation teacher (11.7\,m) and convicting a missing
computation: set encoders cannot perform per-cell matched filtering internally,
and no supervision --- sharper targets, transport losses, or a modern
flow-based posterior head --- teaches what the forward pass cannot represent.
Our recipe hands the computation over: two closed-form matched-filter maps as
input channels, a zero-initialized residual head, and distillation from the
tempered exact posterior. Across three architecture families and nine
seed-runs, every model lands under the 50\,m bar (43.6--49.3\,m) at unchanged
coverage and latency, outperforming a fairness-hardened flow-based neural
posterior estimator (55.5--71.2\,m). Under realistic wind-measurement error the
exact-physics pipeline collapses outright (coverage 0.70) while the distilled
students remain calibrated, and modeling the wind uncertainty in the input maps
recovers a further 12\,m.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
methane monitoring, conformal prediction, simulation-based inference,

\section{Introduction}
Methane delivers roughly $80\times$ the 20-year warming potency of CO$_2$ and
decays within about a decade, making rapid repair of oil-and-gas
\emph{super-emitters} one of the highest-leverage near-term climate actions.
Detection is increasingly solved: UNEP's satellite alert system issued over a
thousand notifications in its first two years --- of which $\sim$1\% saw
documented action \cite{unep}. The missing step is attribution: converting a
detection into a source location trustworthy enough to dispatch a crew. The
EPA Super-Emitter Program makes ``trustworthy enough'' concrete --- within
50\,m \cite{epa} --- and EU methane regulation imposes analogous source-level
duties \cite{eu}. We treat 50\,m as an engineering target.

Continuous monitoring with sparse networks of fixed point sensors is where
attribution must happen: a handful of cheap masts around a facility, always
on, catching the plume as it sweeps across them under naturally meandering
wind. A neural localizer maps readings to a probability map over source
locations, and split conformal prediction \cite{conformal} converts that map
into a region guaranteed to contain the source 90\% of the time. But coverage
is a statement about frequency, not usefulness: a region can be honestly 90\%
and still be too large to act on. This paper measures that gap exactly,
diagnoses its mechanism by elimination, and closes it with a deliberately
simple recipe.

\textbf{Central idea.} When training data come from a simulator, the exact
Bayesian posterior of every training scenario is computable offline. We use it
twice: as a \emph{reference} that turns ``how sharp could a calibrated region
be'' into a number, and as a \emph{teacher}. The novelty is not any component
--- matched filters are 1940s detection theory, distillation and conformal
prediction are standard --- but the diagnosis-driven composition, and the
finding it certifies: \emph{the binding constraint on calibrated localizers is
a computation that must be handed to the network, not a lesson that can be
taught to it.}

\section{Testbed and Exact-Posterior Audit}
\textbf{CH4-T} places a source of unknown location and rate
(10--500\,kg\,h$^{-1}$, log-uniform, bracketing the 100\,kg\,h$^{-1}$
super-emitter threshold) on a $500{\times}500$\,m site sensed by 4--12 masts
(ppm readings, 0.2--3\,ppm noise, dropout). The plume is a steady Gaussian
under known Pasquill--Gifford stability with Briggs open-country dispersion
(coefficients verified against published tables; plume mass flux conserved to
$4{\times}10^{-6}$), driven quasi-statically by a known fluctuating wind
record (1--8\,m\,s$^{-1}$ with AR(1) meander; we interpret steps as 1-min
averages, a 30-min window). As the wind veers, the plume sweeps the masts:
time-of-arrival information reduces weakly-identifiable scenarios from 57\%
(constant wind) to 6\%.

Because the response is closed-form and linear in rate, the exact posterior
over a $64{\times}64$ grid is computable per scenario (adaptive rate
quadrature; float64; normalization error $\sim$$10^{-12}$). A blocking gate
requires conformal prediction applied to the exact posterior itself to
self-calibrate (0.95 observed) --- a gate that caught two real implementation
bugs during development. All models share identical data
(10k/1k/2k/2k train/val/calib/test, at the modal simulation budget of SBI
benchmarks \cite{sbibm}), identical D4 symmetry augmentation, and an
identical randomized-HPD split-conformal wrapper at verified 90\% coverage,
computed in numerically safe tail-mass form. Region sizes are reported as
equal-area circle radii.

\section{Diagnosis by Elimination}
\label{sec:diag}
\textbf{Calibrated $\neq$ actionable.} A DeepSets localizer trained on
one-hot labels (M0) is calibrated (coverage 0.89) with a median radius of
94\,m --- $13\times$ the Bayes limit in radius. The information was in the
readings; the model could not extract it.

\textbf{The teacher is not the bottleneck.} Distilling the exact posterior
requires tempering: the raw near-delta posterior fails outright (134\,m,
\emph{worse than no physics}), while a 0.75-cell blur of it works (74\,m).
Is the blur the ceiling? Run the audit \emph{on the tempered teacher
itself}: it supports 11.7\,m. The student sits $6\times$ above its own
textbook --- the failure is approximation, not supervision content.

\textbf{Supervision cannot fix it.} Annealing the temper toward the raw
posterior makes things worse (87\,m); a debiased Sinkhorn transport loss with
$\varepsilon$-scaling also fails (243\,m; implementation-scoped). Replacing
the categorical head with a flow-based neural posterior estimator
\cite{npe,nsf} --- the current SBI standard, given a $2.5\times$ budget, a
widened head, and full-band conditioning after adversarial review of our own
implementation --- improves the labels-only baseline (62.8--71.2\,m) but
remains far from the limit, and we verified the spline head can express
4.4\,m regions, so its blur is not head-structural.

\textbf{Verdict.} Localizing requires correlating each of 4{,}096 candidate
cells' predicted space--time signature against all readings --- per-cell
matched filtering --- which a pooled set representation cannot carry. No
target and no loss teaches a network what its forward pass cannot compute.

\section{Method: Physics-Guided Posterior Distillation}
The forward model is known; the missing computation has a formula. The recipe
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}) hands it over and lets learning do the rest. For
any base localizer: \textbf{(1)}~compute per cell $c$ the sufficient
statistics $a_c{=}\sum g_c y$, $b_c{=}\sum g_c^2$ of the linear-Gaussian
model ($g_c$ the unit-rate response) and feed two $64{\times}64$ channels,
the matched-filter statistic $z_c{=}a_c/(\sigma\sqrt{b_c})$ and $\log b_c$;
\textbf{(2)}~add a 3-layer, zero-initialized conv head ($\sim$10k parameters,
0.2\% of the base) whose output is summed with the localizer's logits;
\textbf{(3)}~train with cross-entropy to the tempered exact posterior;
\textbf{(4)}~wrap with unchanged split conformal. The maps add no
information --- they are computed from the network's own inputs --- only a
computation; the teacher adds no inference cost; zero-initialization makes
every comparison controlled by construction.

\textbf{Why not just use the teacher?} On this testbed one should --- it is
the measuring instrument, tractable by construction. In deployment it
vanishes: realistic dispersion has no closed form and simulator inversion
costs hours per event \cite{newman} where monitoring needs seconds.
Moreover (\S\ref{sec:robust}) the teacher \emph{breaks} under realistic wind
error while its students do not. And ``the teacher is just part of the
dataset'' is our thesis, not an objection: every simulated training set
already contains its own uncertainty labels; standard training throws them
away.

Table~\ref{tab:main}: every ingredient improves every architecture; all nine
full-recipe seed-runs land under the 50\,m bar; and the spread across
architectures collapses (94-vs-56 without the recipe, 49-vs-44 with it) ---
supervision and inputs, not encoder design, were the binding constraint.
Paired Wilcoxon on per-scenario log region sizes: $p<10^{-200}$; point
accuracy is essentially unchanged --- what changes is the shape of
uncertainty, which is what region size measures. Table~\ref{tab:flow}: the
modern head is a genuine lever (it beats plain distillation without any
physics), our levers transfer onto it monotonically, and it still trails ---
the categorical head's \emph{spatial} physics injection (a conv over the
maps) extracts more than the flow's parametric conditioning at identical
information. The recipe is compared against, not replaced by, the current
standard.

Real anemometers err. Under a 10$^\circ$/10\% observation error the
``exact'' posterior assigns \emph{numerically zero} probability to the true
source in 30\% of scenarios --- no threshold can cover a zero-mass cell, and
the oracle's conformal coverage collapses to 0.70 (Table~\ref{tab:wind}).
The distilled student, trained through the same noise, remains calibrated: a
softmax never says ``impossible,'' and training widens its uncertainty
exactly where wind error demands. Making the physics inputs
uncertainty-aware --- \emph{ensemble maps}, the mean and spread of the
matched-filter maps over eight draws from the wind-error model --- recovers
12\,m ($p{=}10^{-122}$). Amortization is not merely cheap; it is robust to
the misspecification that destroys its own teacher, and uncertainty in
physics inputs must be modeled, not ignored.

\section{Limitations}
CH4-T is an instrument, not a deployment: single steady sources (no
zero-leak or multi-source scenarios), flat terrain without equipment wakes,
known stability, quasi-static stepping, in-distribution marginal coverage.
Rates sit in the super-emitter regime, above the $\leq$7\,kg\,h$^{-1}$ range
of standard METEC continuous-monitoring tests \cite{metec}; localization
granularity matches what such facilities score. The residual factor of
$\sim$6 to the 7\,m limit is unexplained headroom; the transport-loss null is
implementation-scoped; a wind-marginalized Bayes limit under measurement
error is future work (the marginalization factorizes over timesteps, but
reliable quadrature requires bearing-adaptive panels). Field claims require
controlled-release validation; every comparison here is exact because all
models share one verified simulator --- fidelity limits transfer, not the
tables.

\section{Conclusion}
Three numbers organize this work: the physics allows 7\,m, regulators ask
for 50\,m, and a calibrated deep localizer delivers 94\,m. The gap is not
the sensors, the teacher, the loss, or the density estimator --- it is a
classical computation the network cannot rediscover. Handing it over, and
distilling the uncertainty the dataset already contained, brings three
different architectures under the actionability bar at unchanged cost, and
survives the wind-measurement error that breaks exact physics inversion.
The recipe is deliberately simple; the evidence standard --- exact-posterior
audits, blocking self-calibration gates, one-change-at-a-time controls with
documented nulls --- is the part we most hope transfers.
